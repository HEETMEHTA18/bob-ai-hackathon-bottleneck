1. Platform Explanation

Power utilities lose billions annually to preventable failures. The real problem isn't that we can't detect failing equipment — it's that existing tools rank assets by raw probability alone, completely ignoring who loses power if that asset fails and how badly. A small transformer serving a hospital district gets buried below a low-impact rural switch with the same failure score. That's the gap.

GridShield (team name: Bottleneck) fuses three data streams — 48h asset telemetry, regional weather exposure, and historical incident records — into a single composit risk score that ranks 30 monitored assets by operational impact, not just probability. It then converts every ranked risk into a concrete maintenance action and automatically assigns and pre-positions field crews using specialty matching and haversine distance-based ETA.

The platform has four distinct operational layers:

  1. Prediction layer — failure probability at 24h and 72h horizons (XGBoost models trained on synthetic fleet data, with a deterministic mock fallback for demo reliability)
  2. Impact layer — how many customers go dark, how many critical facilities are affected, how many downstream assets cascade if this one fails
  3. Decision layer — composite risk ranking, impact-aware maintenance priorities, crew pre-positioning plan
  4. Advisor layer — a grounded AI copilot (Gemini 2.0 Flash when the key is present, deterministic intent-matched fallback otherwise) that answers natural-language operational questions using only verified backend data

Risk formula (exact implementation from risk_engine.py):

risk_raw = p240.35 × impact0.25 × weather0.15 × criticality0.15 × (1−redundancy)0.10
score = risk_raw × 100 × 1.35  [+age boost if >15 years]
→ normalised 0–100

This is a geometric-mean–style product: every dimension is influential. An asset with high failure probability but minimal customer exposure ranks below a slightly less likely asset that serves 11,000 customers and 10 critical facilities. That is the core operational insight.

7. Top 3 Differentiators

1 Impact-Aware Risk Scoring — Not Just Probability

The composite formula combines five independent dimensions: failure probability, grid impact (customers + facilities + downstream assets + capacity), weather exposure, criticality, and redundancy. A 40%-probability asset serving 11,000 customers ranks ahead of an 80%-probability asset serving 200. This is the central operational insight: consequence × probability, not just probability.

2 Explainable Risk with Deterministic, Reproducible Behavior

Every risk score has a named breakdown: which of the five dimensions drove it and why. The "Why Risky" factors trace back to specific telemetry signals (oil temp, vibration, partial discharge), incident recency, and weather conditions. Because the predictor uses a stable FailurePrediction contract and a deterministic mock, every demo run produces identical, verifiable numbers — no randomness, no hallucination, no "it works on my machine" instability.

3 End-to-End Operational Pipeline — Predict to Crew Dispatch in One Application

Most grid monitoring tools stop at detection. GridShield continues through maintenance planning (specific action, time window, duration) and crew pre-positioning (specialty match, haversine distance, ETA calculation) — all in one application, all driven by the same risk ranking. The scenario simulator extends this to future conditions. The AI advisor makes all of it accessible via natural language. This is a complete operational decision-support system, not a dashboard.

9. Final 5 Judge Takeaways

  1. Impact-aware, not probability-only. The composite risk formula multiplies failure probability by grid impact (customers, facilities, downstream assets), weather, criticality, and redundancy — so the asset that matters most to the grid ranks first, not merely the one most likely to fail.
  2. Explainable at every step. Every risk score has named contributing factors traceable to specific telemetry signals and incident history. An operator can see exactly why TR-1042 scores 98 — 5 specific reasons, all backed by real data.
  3. Complete U1 pipeline in one application. Predict (XGBoost / deterministic mock) → rank → maintenance action with time window → crew dispatch with specialty match and ETA. The platform closes the loop from data to operator action.
  4. Weather-as-risk-multiplier with scenario simulation. Weather is not decorative — it is a live input to the risk score and a scenario dimension. The Severe Storm simulator shows that TR-4060 escalates from "high" to "critical" (+18.4 points), changing crew priority before a storm even arrives.
  5. Built with IBM Bob as engineering partner throughout. Five documented sessions from repository audit through architecture, full backend, complete frontend, 44-test suite, and documentation — with the engineering log available in the repository for judges to review.
